# A self-improvement loop on IKAM (E400)

The owner asked for a recursive self-improvement loop run via IKAM to improve IKAM itself. This plan says what the loop is, what it may change, what it may not, and how it fits the nightly loop net (E386).

## Two levels, one net

- **Level 0, the kernel.** A candidate is one change to the kernel, committed on its own branch (`exp/400-cand-*`). The first version's candidates are one catalog constant changed by one line (`experiments/self-loop/catalog.tsv`, the tunables the experiments README names, as E322 used them). Each is measured by a fixed procedure and kept or closed.
- **Level 1, how the loop picks.** A ranking policy orders each round's candidates: a weight per feature read from the loop's own history (how much this constant moved the north score before, how often it improved or worsened it, whether the new value lies on the side of a value that helped, the size of the step, whether it was never tried, its usual test time). After a round, a meta step proposes variants of the policy and adopts one only if it ranks held-out rounds better. The meta step also changes its own search radius (wider after an adoption, narrower after a rejection), so the search that improves the picker is itself adjusted by results.
- **One score for level 1:** changes kept per test-hour, `fixed.kept_per_hour`: kept changes found in the first half of a pool's total test time, and the area under the kept-against-hours curve. Test time is everything the machine spends on a candidate: build, benches, tests.
- **State on IKAM.** Everything is a firing of the `self` net (`experiments/self-loop/self.net`, run by `ikam net` since E401) on an FsStore, ref `loop/self`. Candidates go through `propose`, `start` (holds the cpu token), `finish` (output: the measured result) and `keep` (to `waiting`) or `close`; `merge` is a human transition the loop never fires. Policies go through `variant` (output: the policy and its held-out score) and `adopt` or `reject`; the policy in use is `retire`d when another is adopted. The loop reads its history back from the firings and keeps no other state, so a killed round resumes from the store and every step replays (E269, E386).

## What the loop cannot change

In `experiments/self-loop/fixed.py`, which the loop imports and never writes:

- the scoring: the two scores, their tolerances and the keep rule (north better beyond 0.5% on the north-only run and both full runs, cost not worse beyond the larger of 2% and the round's base spread on both, as E322's analyze.py applies the README rule), the hard gates (the bench's byte-exact reconstruction and fsck, then `cargo test --release` for a kept change);
- the evaluator that judges a policy;
- the guards: a candidate's diff must be exactly the catalog line with only its value changed (`check_diff`), protected paths (tests, `examples/bench.rs` and its common code, the catalog, `fixed.py`, `Cargo.toml` and the lock file, `.github`, the experiments README) are hashed at the start of a round and checked before every verdict, branches the loop writes must match `exp/400-(self-loop|cand-*)`, and a stored policy may hold only weights, a radius and a seed (`check_policy`).
- main: the loop pushes nothing and merges nothing. Kept candidates wait in `waiting` for the owner's merge, a human transition.

`experiments/self-loop/guard_test.py` checks that each guard refuses what it should.

Not a candidate: `ARCHIVE_READS` in the bench (E322 tried it), because it changes the north score's weights, which is the scoring.

## The test procedure

North first, because the north score is deterministic (bytes only) and a north-only run takes about 104 s here, against 232 s for a full run:

1. build the bench (about 30 s incremental);
2. the three north corpora alone; worse beyond 0.5% closes the candidate, flat closes it too (a flat-north change could only be kept for a cost win, and on a cloud machine the cost score's own spread is 2-5%, E322);
3. north better: two full runs, the keep rule on both;
4. kept: `cargo test --release`.

## How it plugs into the nights

The nights' net (E386's `loop_drive`, the trip-week plan) drives agent-written experiments; this loop drives machine-made candidates. They can share a night without sharing state:

- A night gives the loop a slot in its plan: one pick, `self-loop round`, whose `finish` output is the round's score. The night net's `cpu` token is held for the round, so the two never bench at once.
- The loop's policy can rank the night's own picks too once those picks record the same features (constant or mechanism, test time, verdict); that is the next step, not this version.
- Kept candidates are options like any other night's keeps: they wait for the owner.

## Limits of the first version

- Candidates are one-line constant changes from a fixed catalog, not code an agent writes. An agent-made candidate would enter as a `propose` firing with its diff as the output, through the same guards (protected paths, own branch), but the one-line rule would have to become a path rule.
- The seed history is small (22 results from E322, E347 and E348, test time modelled), so the first policy is fitted to two held-out rounds and may be overfitted. Round 1 measures it on candidates it has not seen.
- A round measures its whole pool, so the fixed ranking and the policy can be scored on the same results; a production round would stop at its budget.

## Round 2: the loop as configuration, and what it knows (E401)

The owner asked that self-improvement be something configured into the executable system, not a feature tied into IKAM, and recalled the older IKAM idea of collaborative development: systems of wide use that people share. Round 2 makes the loop a configuration any store can run and its knowledge a tree any store can merge, and takes five ideas from Cairn, the Darwin Gödel Machine and ShinkaEvolve (ideas only; Cairn's AGPL code is not used).

### Configured, not built in

- `ikam net NETFILE [--ref REF] fire T TOKEN [OUTFILE] | history | marking | enabled | check` runs any Petri net from a net file (the `Net::parse` format) on a store, one firing a call. It knows nothing about self-improvement: it refuses to fire a human transition, refuses a firing replayed with other output, and checks a store by replay. The self loop's net is the text file `self.net`; the night net could be another.
- `loop.json` names the steps: where candidates come from (`catalog`), how a parent is picked (`archive` or `main`), what counts as a repeat (`exact`, `exact+hints`, `embed`), how candidates are ranked (`policy`), the ref, the budget and whether to re-plan on news only. A step is a module under `steps/`; another loop swaps one by naming another module.
- `fixed.check_config` refuses a config that names anything else, or any key for measuring, scoring, verdicts, tolerances or keeping: those stay in `fixed.py`, which no config or step replaces. `self.net` and `loop.json` are protected paths, so a round cannot change its own net or config.

### The five ideas

1. **A findings graph in IKAM** (`findings.py`, after Cairn). What the loop knows is a tree: `facts/` (one per measured candidate: its changes from the base commit, its parent, scores, verdict and the firing it came from), `hints/` (short notes with the facts they rest on: a constant that does not move north at a commit, a change that alone makes north worse, a catalog used up) and `intents/` (proposed, not yet measured). It is built from the net's firings, committed with `ikam commit DIR --ref loop/findings` and read back from IKAM before each round, so the proposer reads the graph, not the experiment log. Every file is named by a hash of its content: two stores' graphs merge by union, and the same name with other content is refused. This is the sharing the older IKAM aimed at: a loop's findings are a tree another person's loop can merge, read and build on.
2. **Re-plan only on news.** With `"replan": "news"`, the meta step runs only when the graph has a kept or contradicting fact or a hint it has not seen; a failed or held run is not news.
3. **Reopen, don't edit.** A kept candidate that a later finding reverses (the same constant set back, and kept) is reopened: a `reopen` firing moves its token out of `waiting` and adds a contradiction fact linked to the finding. The kept fact is never changed.
4. **Weaker variants as parents** (Darwin Gödel Machine). A candidate's parent is picked from the archive of measured variants at the base, weighted by `sigmoid(-dn / 0.5%) / (1 + children)`, so a variant slightly worse than its base still gets children and one with many children gets fewer. The parent is checked out, one more catalog line is changed, and the child is measured against the base by the same fixed procedure. `fixed.check_chain` refuses a parent that differs from the base by anything other than catalog lines set to values the catalog tries.
5. **Skip near-repeats** (ShinkaEvolve). Before a candidate is tested, the novelty step skips the same set of changes already measured at the base, a change the hints rule out, and a set equal to a measured one once its non-moving constants are dropped. The `embed` mode adds a cosine check with IKAM's bge-small embedder; it needs the model's weights, which this cloud workspace cannot download, so round 2 uses `exact+hints`.

`loop_test.py` checks the mechanisms that a round only shows when they happen (reopen, merge and conflict, novelty, the refused merge firing) on a scratch store.

### Round 2 results

Round r2 ran on the same base (main 0a6f0eb, north 0.30603, cost median 0.2395) with `loop.json` as above: parents from the archive, `exact+hints` novelty, re-plan on news.

- 30 candidates of two to four changes each (2.7 on average), 1.24 test-hours, 0 kept: 16 held, 13 worse, 1 failed a gate. Kept per test-hour is 0, as in r1.
- The one candidate past the north threshold, `seed` 24 on `group-shortlist` 16, `gap-diff` 256 and `related-min` 4096 (north 1.22% better, both full runs the same, cost within tolerance), would have been kept and failed `cargo test`. Rerun by hand, the failure is real, not flaky: with `SEED` (the matcher's seed length, also its minimum match) at 24, `repack::tests::induction_shares_one_template_across_a_cluster` no longer finds one template for its 30 invoices. A round now records the failing test names with the verdict.
- Each pick skipped 17 to 22 options as exact repeats or ruled out by hints, so no candidate was a set already measured at the base.
- Parents: all 30 were built on a candidate that had held or been worse, 16 of them on a round-2 candidate; E400 would only ever have started from main.
- Re-plan on news: the meta step after r2 found 24 unseen graph entries, proposed variants and rejected them (held-out 0.461, no better variant); run again with nothing new, it fired nothing.
- No kept result was reversed, so nothing was reopened; `loop_test.py` shows the reopen on a scratch store.
- The findings graph after r2 is 96 entries, 47 KB, about 5% of `docs/experiments/log.md` (951 KB), and it is what the proposer read.
- Store check: 336 firings, replay equal, every output readable, fsck clean, no merge firing.
